2 Executive Summary of Activities
This quarter addressed thehierarchy problemin fine-grained vehicle classification on the pro-
ject dataset UFPR-VeSV: a prediction is only usable for retrieval whentype,makeandmodel
aresimultaneouslycorrect, which independent per-task heads do not guarantee. Two soluti-
ons were consolidated: Hierarchical Constrained Decoding (HCD), a training-free step that
restricts the decision to the 225 tuples observed in the project ontology, and joint-class promp-
ting, which replaces the three marginal prompt banks of a vision-language model (VLM) by
a single 225-way prompt.
On eight supervised backbones, HCD raised simultaneous accuracy by+2.86to+10.10
percentage points (pp) without breaking an already-correct triplet. On 16 zero-shot VLMs
over the same five folds, the joint prompt beat paired HCD in only three backbones and lost
in the other thirteen, so the decoder must be chosen per backbone. Supervising the joint
head changes the regime: visual LoRA reached 94.78% (PE-Core-bigG) and 93.93% (SigLIP2-
gopt), at 51k and 300k images/h on one RTX A5000. Confidence wasmeasured, not assumed:
calibrated HCD lowered the expected calibration error (ECE) on the six stronger supervised
backbones, but on the zero-shot VLMs it improved test negative log-likelihood (NLL) in all
16 while improving ECE in only 8. Temperature scaling brought the tuned LoRA checkpoints
to0.85–1.66% ECE, the best accuracy/calibration combination obtained so far.

3 Technical Progress and Methodology
3.1 Methodology and Implementation
Task, data and metric.UFPR-VeSV contains 24,945 images of 16,297 vehicles/plates
(19,573 visible, 5,372 infrared, IR) labelled with 14type, 26makeand 136modelclasses. Of
the 49,504 Cartesian combinations only225 tuplesoccur, making the label space a two-
level hierarchy [4, 5] under the surveillance conditions also reported for vehicle colour in [9].
The metric throughout issimultaneous accuracy(all three attributes correct on the same
image),notthe average of the three marginal accuracies. We use the five canonical folds
0..4once each, with train/val/test separated by plate; the tables report absolute results
(mean±standard deviation over the folds), without significance testing at this stage.
HCD: constrained decoding of marginals.Given the three marginal logit vectors for
an imagex, raw HCD scores every admissible tupleτ∈T,|T|= 225, and keeps the best one:

This applies the label-relation constraint of [4] as pure post-processing — no retraining and
no extra visual forward pass, unlike consistency-aware training [5]. The calibrated variant
HCD-Cfits two relative task weights and one joint temperature on thevalidationsplit of
each fold by NLL; the temperature alone cannot change theargmax, but the weights can.
Joint 225-class prompting.For each valid tuple we build five textual descriptions,
average their text embeddings and compare the image against the resulting 225 class vectors.
Sixteen public checkpoints were evaluated zero-shot through OpenCLIP [2]: CLIP [10], Si-
gLIP 1/2 [14, 11], PE-Core [1] and MetaCLIP 2 [3]. Each image is encodedonceand the
same visual embedding feeds both the marginal banks (then HCD) and the joint bank, so the
two decisions are paired by checkpoint, image, pre-processing, fold and ontology; the usual
“3×cheaper” argument for joint classification therefore doesnotapply here.
Supervised joint head and calibration protocol.Four backbones were also adapted
to the 225 classes on the same folds (ten epochs, training ontrain, evaluation ontest):
CoOp [15] with 16 shared context tokens and frozen visual tower, and visual LoRA [7] with
rank 16,α= 32, Q/V adaptation, batch 64 and frozen text tower. ECE is measured on
the top-1 confidence of thecomplete tupleover 15 equal-width bins [8], and temperature
scaling (TS) [6] and HCD-C were fittedonlyon validation. Since both objectives are NLL
and neither minimises ECE directly [12], ECE is reported as measured, with NLL as the
complementary criterion.

3.3 Confidence Calibration
Supervised reference.On the historical suite, HCD-C reduced test ECE on the six stronger
backbones (EfficientNet6.02→1.08%, Swin-T6.10→1.07%, ResNet-1015.94→0.84%, ViT
6.99→0.90%) but not on the two detectors (YOLO11n3.97→5.98%), where raw HCD was
already the best-calibrated variant even though NLL/Brier still favour HCD-C. The joint
NLL ladder is consistent: EfficientNet0.7740(raw marginal product)→0.5843(raw HCD)
→0.4150(HCD-C), and Swin-T0.7768→0.6078→0.3969.
Zero-shot VLMs.Closing the 80 missing validation runs (16 models×5 folds) gives a
differentpicture(Table4). Direct+TSisbettercalibratedthanHCD-Cinall16backbonesand
improves on the raw direct classifier in 13 of them; the three exceptions (SigLIP2-L, SigLIP1-
L, CLIP-bigG) are precisely the backbones whose raw joint confidence was already closest to
calibrated, and TS was fitted for NLL, not for ECE. HCD-C reduced ECE against raw HCD
in only8 of 16backbones while reducing test NLL inall 16(SigLIP2-gopt: NLL1.31→1.06
with ECE5.96→9.47%; SigLIP2-SO400M: NLL1.47→1.21with ECE5.79→9.22%).
Reweighting the tasks also moves theargmax, so global top-1 calibration does not follow the
multiclass NLL objective. This does not establish a general superiority of the direct classifier,
since its accuracy differs from that of HCD-C and tail/IR confidence was not re-evaluated
here.
